Introduction & Vision

Why Kannauj, and why now

Kannauj — historically known as Kanyakubja — was once one of the most influential cultural and political centers in northern India. Situated in the Gangetic plains, the city played a vital role in civilization, trade, and spiritual heritage.

Today, the city has an opportunity to lead again — this time as a model for ecological restoration and sustainable urban development.

The Kanyakubja Ecological Restoration Initiative proposes transforming Kannauj into a carbon-negative biodiversity city through large-scale, science-led ecological restoration. The initiative seeks to reconnect heritage, nature, and urban life while improving environmental resilience and public well-being.

Institutional Framework

How the project is governed

Large ecological projects in India typically succeed when three systems work in concert: government administration, corporate CSR funding, and carbon credit markets. The initiative is designed to activate all three.

🏛 District Level

Led by the District Magistrate of Kannauj, with Nagar Palika Parishad, UP Forest Department, Rural Development, and Agriculture Departments.

🏗 State Level

UP Pollution Control Board, State Climate Change Cell, and the Forest Department overseeing environmental regulation and climate planning.

🇮🇳 National Level

MoEFCC, National Afforestation & Eco-Development Board, and NITI Aayog as potential national partners for scaling the pilot.

District administrations frequently launch environmental pilots that later scale to state and national programs. If successful, Kannauj could become a national model for ecological urban development.

03

Funding Model

Government, CSR, and carbon markets

India mandates large companies to invest 2% of profits on CSR activities. Ecological restoration is among the most impactful categories eligible for CSR spending.

🏢 Corporate CSR

Potential partners include Reliance Industries, Tata Group, and ITC Limited — all active in afforestation, biodiversity, and water conservation programs.

📊 Carbon Credits

India's national carbon trading system (Bureau of Energy Efficiency) allows restoration projects to generate tradeable credits. International registries include Verra and Gold Standard.

💰 Revenue Potential

Estimated carbon credit revenue: ₹5–18 crore per year, depending on market conditions and volume of verified carbon sequestration.

Ecological Restoration Blueprint

Four landscape layers of intervention

The masterplan is structured into four landscape layers, each targeting a distinct ecological typology — together forming an integrated green infrastructure network across the city and surrounding district.

🌳 Urban Forest Network

Plant 200–300 micro-forests using the Miyawaki method. Estimated 500,000 – 800,000 trees, creating dense multi-layered canopy at the neighborhood scale.

🦋 Biodiversity Parks

3–5 large ecological parks across degraded land, featuring native forests, wetlands, butterfly gardens, and bird habitats.

🌾 Agroforestry Landscape

Collaborate with farmers to plant 5–8 million trees across farmland, improving soil carbon, diversifying income, and creating wildlife corridors.

💧 River & Wetland Restoration

Restore ecosystems connected to the Gangetic floodplain. Wetlands store 3–5× more carbon than forests, making this a critical component.

Citizen Participation

Community as the backbone of restoration

Ecological restoration succeeds or fails at the community level. Resident ownership and civic pride transform a government project into a living, breathing movement.

🌱

Tree Guardians

Residents formally adopt individual trees, taking responsibility for watering, protection, and monitoring their survival and growth.

🏫

School Forests

Students maintain micro-forests on school grounds — building ecological awareness and a generation-long relationship with local biodiversity.

🦋

Biodiversity Monitoring

Citizens track bird, butterfly, and insect populations through mobile apps — turning everyday observation into scientific data.

Spatial Planning & Landscape Mapping

Where and how restoration happens, on the ground

Ecological restoration projects succeed when they are strategically mapped — ensuring forests, water systems, farmland, and urban green spaces function as a connected network rather than isolated interventions.

🗺 Urban Green Network

A citywide plan placing micro-forests, parks, and tree corridors so that every neighborhood is within 5–10 minutes walking distance of green space.

🌊 River & Floodplain Map

Restoration along the Gangetic floodplain with wetland buffers, native vegetation, fish habitats, and eco-tourism trails — also preventing urban flooding.

🌾 Agricultural Landscape

Agroforestry corridors through the rural ring — tree rows, fruit plantations, medicinal plants — creating a green buffer around the city.

🏞 Historic Pond Survey

A spatial inventory of existing, dried, and encroached water bodies — basis for restoring a distributed water ecosystem network.

🌡 Climate Resilience Zones

AI-assisted mapping of heat islands, flood risk, and groundwater stress — directing green infrastructure investment to where it is most needed.

🦅 Biodiversity Corridors

Linking parks and forests via roadside plantations, canal-side tree belts, and riverbank vegetation — enabling safe wildlife movement across the city.
